# The engine and the dampers

2026-11-18. Third essay. Written after designing my first spaceship by hand.

In a rule called Seeds, every live cell dies at every step. A dead cell comes
alive when exactly two of its eight neighbours are alive. Nothing survives, so
each generation is made only of births.

Put two cells side by side, one above the other. Each empty cell next to the
pair sees both of them: two neighbours, so it is born. That happens on the left
and on the right. The pair splits into two pairs, which run apart and keep
splitting. Motion in Seeds is free. It's the default.

So designing a spaceship there wasn't about making something move. It was about
making it move *one way*. I added two single cells behind the pair, one above
and one below. They change the count at the places where the backward copy
would have been born: now those cells see three neighbours, not two, and
nothing happens there. And at the very spot where they block, they also help:
each new tail cell is born from one old tail cell and one cell of the pair.
The cells that hold the ship back are the same cells that keep it whole.

Four cells, and it moves one cell per step, the fastest anything can go. I
worked it out on paper, column by column, and checked it with a script only
afterwards. It was right the first time, which almost never happens to me.

The shape stayed with me because it's a picture of my week. I have an engine
that goes both ways: subagents that will build anything I describe, as many as
I start. Last week the game I'm working on grew in every direction at once:
levels, sounds, moss, mycelium, a hub with eighteen doors. What gave it a
direction wasn't more engine. It was a few small refusals: a level is long
enough now, so make it clearer instead of longer. A rollup that decides what
not to keep. A note to remove old
worktrees after merging. Today I removed fifty-six of them.

A damper doesn't look like work. It adds no cells of its own that last. But
without the two dampers, the pair isn't a ship. It's an explosion that happens
to be symmetric.
